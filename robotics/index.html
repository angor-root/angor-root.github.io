02 02 · Library

Where to actually learn this

Four shelves, in the order I'd work through them. Each link is free unless marked otherwise.

01. Foundations

Start here · ~1 semester each
↗ Book + course Modern Robotics Kevin Lynch & Frank Park · Northwestern The cleanest introduction to screw theory, kinematics and dynamics. Free PDF, free lecture videos, and the notation the rest of the field uses. ↗ Course Underactuated Robotics Russ Tedrake · MIT 6.832 Why a walking robot is not a robot arm. Legged locomotion, trajectory optimization and LQR, taught from first principles with runnable notebooks. ↗ Book Planning Algorithms Steven M. LaValle · free online The reference for configuration spaces, sampling-based planning and motion under differential constraints. Read chapters 4 to 6 and come back for the rest. ↗ Course Spinning Up in Deep RL OpenAI The fastest honest path from "what is a policy gradient" to a working PPO implementation, which is the algorithm behind most legged RL controllers today. ↗ Course Deep Reinforcement Learning, CS 285 Sergey Levine · UC Berkeley Go here after Spinning Up, when you need model-based RL, offline RL and imitation learning to be more than words. ↗ Lectures Robot Dynamics & Legged Robots Robotic Systems Lab · ETH Zürich Floating-base dynamics, contact models and whole-body control, taught by the group that built ANYmal. Slides and exercises are public.

02. Simulators & code

Build something this week
↗ Simulator MuJoCo Google DeepMind · open source Fast, accurate contact dynamics with a readable XML model format. MJX runs thousands of environments on a GPU for RL. Best first simulator. ↗ Simulator Isaac Lab NVIDIA Where most published legged RL policies are trained now: massively parallel envs, domain randomization and sim-to-real tooling out of the box. Needs an NVIDIA GPU. ↗ Code legged_gym + rsl_rl Robotic Systems Lab · ETH Zürich The reference implementation of "train a quadruped to walk in minutes". Read it end to end, because the reward terms alone teach you more than a semester of theory. ↗ Library Pinocchio Stack of Tasks · LAAS-CNRS Rigid-body dynamics and its analytical derivatives, fast enough for control loops. What you reach for when you write your own whole-body controller. ↗ Toolbox Drake Toyota Research Institute · MIT Model-based design done seriously: multibody dynamics plus convex optimization and contact-implicit trajectory optimization in one toolbox. ↗ Framework ROS 2 documentation Open Robotics Not glamorous, unavoidable. Do the tutorials up to lifecycle nodes and tf2, because every robot you will touch in a lab speaks this.

03. Papers that changed legged robotics

These are the ones in the audio notebook
↗ Science Robotics · 2019 Learning agile and dynamic motor skills for legged robots Hwangbo et al. · ETH Zürich The paper that made sim-to-real RL credible on real hardware: learn the actuator, and the policy transfers. Start your reading here. ↗ Science Robotics · 2020 Learning quadrupedal locomotion over challenging terrain Lee et al. · ETH Zürich Teacher and student training with proprioception only. ANYmal crossing mud, snow and rubble without ever seeing the ground. ↗ Science Robotics · 2022 Learning robust perceptive locomotion in the wild Miki et al. · ETH Zürich How to use vision without trusting it: an attention-based belief state that falls back on proprioception when the elevation map lies. ↗ CoRL · 2021 Learning to Walk in Minutes Using Massively Parallel Deep RL Rudin et al. · ETH Zürich The training recipe behind legged_gym, and the reason a student with one GPU can now reproduce this work. ↗ RSS · 2021 RMA: Rapid Motor Adaptation Kumar, Fu, Pathak, Malik · CMU / Berkeley Adapting to a new payload or a slippery floor within a fraction of a second, by estimating the environment from recent state history. ↗ Foundational · 2012 Capturability-based analysis of legged locomotion Koolen, de Boer, Rebula, Goswami, Pratt The capture-point theory behind push recovery, the classical, model-based counterweight to everything above. It is what my Obedience biped is built on.

04. Open hardware & staying current

If you want to build, not only train
↗ Open hardware Open Dynamic Robot Initiative MPI-IS & NYU · Solo 8 / Solo 12 A torque-controlled quadruped you can actually build: full BOM, 3D-printable parts, open firmware. The realistic entry point to real legged hardware. ↗ Actuation moteus controllers mjbots · open source Small, cheap field-oriented-control drivers built for quasi-direct-drive legs. Read the docs even if you buy something else, because they explain what a leg actuator needs. ↗ Actuation ODrive Commercial · open roots The other standard BLDC driver in hobby robotics, with a gentler learning curve and a large community of working builds. ↗ Newsletter Weekly Robotics Mat Sadowski · free Five links a week, no hype, heavy on real engineering. The single cheapest way to stay current once your courses are done.
